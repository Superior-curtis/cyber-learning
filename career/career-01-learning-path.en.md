# Learning Path & Practice Grounds

> 📅 2026-08-05 · Getting Started
> This book goes from basics to hands-on, but finishing it is not the goal — practicing it is. This article threads the whole book into a learning route and shows where to find free practice grounds and community.

---

You have read this far — from `found-01`'s "what is security" to `blue-07`'s zero trust. You have walked most of this book's knowledge map. But security has an old saying: **finishing the reading is not the goal; practicing is.**

This article threads the whole book into a learning route that is yours, and shows where to find free practice grounds.

## Threading the book into a route

The articles are not scattered points; they follow a deliberate order:

| Phase | Book chapters | What you are doing |
|---|---|---|
| Build foundations | `found-*`, `linux-*`, `net-*`, `crypto-*` | Understand how the world works |
| Systematic knowledge | `secplus-*` | Use the Security+ outline to check for gaps |
| Offense & defense thinking | `recon-*`, `password-*`, `web-*`, `cve-*` | Know what attacks look like, and how to defend |
| Hands-on tools | `kali-*` | Turn knowledge into tool feel |
| Practical practice | `labs-*` | Actually do it, in an isolated environment |
| Defensive integration | `blue-*` | Move from single points to a system |

**Suggested route: finish "foundations → offense → hands-on" first, then circle back for "defense."** With the offense perspective, defense has an object.

## Free practice grounds

"Practicing" needs a place. All of these are free or low-cost:

| Ground | What you practice |
|---|---|
| Your own lab | `lab-01-build-your-lab` — safest, freest |
| CTF platforms | The CTFs mentioned in `lab-03-ctf-101` — challenges and community |
| Deliberately vulnerable targets | `lab-02-vulnerable-targets` — DVWA, Metasploitable |
| Course-bundled labs | Many free courses come with practice environments |

**The single most important thing: practice on a schedule.** A few fixed hours weekly beats an occasional all-nighter. Security is a "feel" trade, and feel is built with time.

## Going with a community

Practicing alone gets you lost. Two suggestions:

* **Write up your solutions**: `lab-09-walkthrough-ethics` shows how — writing is itself the best review.
* **Find partners or a community**: discuss challenges, share notes, set each other puzzles. Companions make learning less lonely.

## A mindset for growth

Finally, a few mindsets that matter for growth:

* **Not knowing is normal**: security is enormous; nobody knows it all. Admitting you do not know is the start of learning.
* **Hands-on first**: reading ten tool articles is weaker than running one.
* **Teaching is the most efficient learning**: if you can explain it, you really know it.
