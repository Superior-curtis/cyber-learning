# Certifications & Careers

---

"Should I get certified?" — every aspiring security professional asks this sooner or later. This article gives you no single yes-or-no, but helps you see certifications clearly: what they are, what they are not, and how to place them on your career map.

## What certifications mean, and their limits

A certification's value in one line: **it is the business card, not the ability.**

* ✅ Benefits: a first filter in resume screening, a framework for systematic learning, a hard requirement for some roles.
* ⚠️ Limit: passing one cert does not equal hands-on ability. What interviewers really want to see is what you *can do*, not what you *memorized*.

The best mindset: **treat preparing for a cert as systematic review.** It forces you to fill the gaps in your knowledge map, but hands-on ability still comes from the practice grounds in `career-01-learning-path`.

## Common certifications

| Cert | Positioning | For |
|---|---|---|
| CompTIA Security+ | Entry, broad but shallow | Beginners building the knowledge skeleton |
| CEH | Entry red-team view | People wanting to meet the attack surface first |
| OSCP | Hands-on penetration testing | People wanting to prove they can really do it |
| CISSP | Senior management & governance | People heading toward security management |

> Each cert only represents its own positioning. Security+ says "I know these terms," OSCP says "I can act in an authorized environment," CISSP says "I understand governance and risk." None of them means "everything."

## Career paths

Security is not one job; it is several parallel tracks. Common branches:

| Track | What it does | In this book |
|---|---|---|
| Blue team / defense | Monitoring, hardening, response | The whole `blue-*` series |
| Red team / testing | Authorized penetration testing | `recon-*`, `kali-*`, `labs-*` |
| Audit / compliance | Checking against requirements | `secplus-06-governance-risk` |
| Security engineering | Building security into systems | `web-05-securing-web-apps` |
| Security management | Resource and risk decisions | `secplus-06-governance-risk` |

**You do not need to choose up front.** Most people spend a year or two building technical depth, then deepen along the track that fits their interest and the market.

## Certifications vs practice: both hands

Practical advice:

1. **Practice first, then certify**: certs mean more — and are easier — once you have hands-on foundations.
2. **Use certs to fill the map**: let the preparation process cover the corners `career-01-learning-path` did not reach.
3. **Certs are only the start**: what actually gets you hired is demonstrable ability — work, practice records, hands-on experience.

> Watch out for "certificate hoarding." A stack of certs with no practice is seen through at a glance; conversely, a solid practice record is often more convincing than a piece of paper. Substance first, certs are a plus; certs alone are naked.
